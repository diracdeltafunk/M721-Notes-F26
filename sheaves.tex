\chapter{Sheaves}

\section{Introduction}

\startlecture[Aug 25, 2026]

Functions\footnote{For now, in this introduction, we mean simply functions between sets, i.e. morphisms in $\Set$.} are arguably the most fundamental concept in mathematics. A key feature of functions is that they may be \emph{restricted}: given a function $f : X \to Y$ and a subset $X' \subseteq X$, we can produce a function
\[f|_{X'} : X' \to Y\]
defined simply by $f|_{X'}(x) = f(x)$. Equivalently, $f|_{X'}$ is the composite
\[X' \hookrightarrow X \xrightarrow{f} Y.\]
Of course, unless $X' = X$, the restriction $f|_{X'}$ holds less information than $f$ -- it no longer remembers the behavior of $f$ on the complement $X - X'$. However, the pair of functions $(f|_{X'}, f|_{X-X'})$ \emph{is} enough to recover the entire data of $f$; indeed we have
\[f(x) = \begin{cases} f|_{X'}(x) &: x \in X' \\ f|_{X-X'}(x) &: x \notin X' \end{cases}\]
for all $x \in X$. There is no more data in $f$ than what it does on $X'$ and its complement $X - X'$. This is a special case of the following more general slogan.

\begin{slogan}\label{slogan:functions are globally determined by local behavior}
    Functions are globally determined by their local behavior.
\end{slogan}

This is only a slogan, so the terms ``global'' and ``local'' (and even ``function'') here are meant to be interpreted loosely. The central aim of this chapter is to develop the understanding that functions are \emph{precisely} those things which are globally determined by their local behavior. The desired understanding is \emph{unrigorous}, i.e. we aim to develop a certain mathematical intuition for what it means to be ``globally determined by local behavior'', and an intuition for what it means that functions are precisely those mathematical objects with this property. As with any goal of this flavor, we must develop the desired intuition by instantiating it in precise theorems.

We can summarize our first example by noticing we have an isomorphism
\[\Hom(X,Y) \cong \Hom(X',Y) \times \Hom(X - X', Y)\]
for any sets $X$ and $Y$ and any subset $X' \subseteq X$. This is a special case of the universal property of coproducts, and more importantly for our purposes, it is also a special case of a \emph{sheaf condition}. We proceed by analyzing a more general situation.

Let $X'$ and $X''$ be subsets of $X$. Then, given a function $f : X \to Y$, we may form the restrictions $f|_{X'}$ and $f|_{X''}$. This gives a map
\begin{align*}
    \Hom(X, Y) & \to \Hom(X',Y) \times \Hom(X'',Y) \\
    f          & \mapsto (f|_{X'}, f|_{X''})
\end{align*}
which in general is neither injective nor surjective. However, it is easy to understand precisely the failures of both of these conditions.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:injectivity of restriction maps}
    The map
    \[
        \Hom(X, Y) \to \Hom(X',Y) \times \Hom(X'',Y)
    \]
    is injective for all sets $Y$ if and only if $X' \cup X'' = X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Elementary.
\end{proof}

Note that this map being injective is the same as saying that $f$ is always reconstructible from the data $(f|_{X'}, f|_{X''})$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:image of restriction maps}
    When $X = X' \cup X''$, the image of the map
    \[
        \Hom(X, Y) \to \Hom(X',Y) \times \Hom(X'',Y)
    \]
    is precisely the set
    \[
        \{(f',f'') \in \Hom(X',Y) \times \Hom(X'', Y) : f'|_{X' \cap X''} = f''|_{X' \cap X''}\}.
    \]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Elementary.
\end{proof}

To summarize all of the above: if $X'$ and $X''$ together cover $X$, then the data of a function on $X$ is equivalent to a pair of functions (one on $X'$ and one on $X''$) which agree on the overlap $X' \cap X''$. In other words, whenever $X = X' \cup X''$, we have a pullback square

We finish with an instantiation of our main goal: to understand that functions are \emph{precisely} those things which are determined globally by their local behavior.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:sheaves are representable}
    Let $\cal{F} : \Set^\op \to \Set$ be a functor satisfying the following ``sheaf condition'':
    \begin{description}
        \item[$(\star)$] For any set $X$ and any collection of subsets $\{X_\alpha\}_\alpha$ of $X$, if $X = \bigcup_\alpha X_\alpha$ then
              \[\cal{F}(X) \to \prod_\alpha \cal{F}(X_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \cal{F}(X_\alpha \cap X_\beta)\]
              is an equalizer diagram in $\Set$, where the maps above are given by applying $\cal{F}$ to the inclusions (as in \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:representables are sheaves}).
    \end{description}
    Then $\cal{F}$ is representable, i.e. $\cal{F} \cong \Hom({-},Y)$ for some set $Y$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Set $Y = \cal{F}(\pt)$. We first note that $\varnothing = \bigcup \varnothing$, whence we have an equalizer diagram of the form
    \[\cal{F}(\varnothing) \to \pt \rightrightarrows \pt,\]
    showing that $\cal{F}(\varnothing) \cong \pt$. Here we have used the fact that $\pt$ is terminal in $\Set$ (and empty products are terminal objects).

    Now let $X$ be an arbitrary set, and write $X = \bigcup_{x \in X} \{x\}$. Then we have an equalizer diagram
    \[\cal{F}(X) \to \prod_{x \in X} \cal{F}(\{x\}) \rightrightarrows \prod_{x, x' \in X} \cal{F}(\{x\} \cap \{x'\}).\]
    We note that $\cal{F}(\{x\} \cap \{x'\}) = \cal{F}(\varnothing) \cong \pt$ unless $x = x'$. Therefore we have an equalizer diagram of the form
    \[\cal{F}(X) \to \prod_{x \in X} \cal{F}(\{x\}) \rightrightarrows \prod_{x \in X} \cal{F}(\{x\})\]
    where the two parallel arrows are equal\footnote{Check!}. We conclude that the map
    \[\cal{F}(X) \to \prod_{x \in X} \cal{F}(\{x\})\]
    is an isomorphism. This allows us to construct an element
    \[i \in \cal{F}(Y) \cong \prod_{y \in Y} \cal{F}(\{y\}) \cong \prod_{y \in Y} \cal{F}(\pt) = \prod_{y \in Y} Y\]
    from the tuple
    \[(y)_{y \in Y},\]
    where the isomorphism in the line above is the product of the isomorphisms $\cal{F}(\{y\}) \to \cal{F}(\pt)$ obtained by applying $\cal{F}$ to the unique map $\pt \to \{y\}$.

\section{Sheaves on Spaces}

Now, let $X$ be a topological space. We are often interested in continuous functions on $X$. Continuous functions also have the property that they are determined globally by their local behavior: in this context, ``local'' is best interpreted as ``on an open set''. That is: a continuous function on $X$ is uniquely determined by its restrictions to the pieces of an open cover of $X$, and a collection of continuous functions on the pieces of an open cover of $X$ comes from a continuous function on $X$ if and only if the continuous functions agree on pairwise intersections of pieces of the open cover. It is important that we restrict to open subsets here! For example, consider the functions
\begin{align*}
    f_1 : (-\infty,0) & \to \RR   & f_2 : [0,\infty) & \to \RR    \\
    x                 & \mapsto 0 & x                & \mapsto 1.
\end{align*}
We see that $f_1$ and $f_2$ are continuous, and we have
\[(-\infty,0) \cup [0,\infty) = \RR \qquad \qquad (-\infty,0) \cap [0,\infty) = \varnothing,\]
but there is no continuous function $f : \RR \to \RR$ such that $f|_{(-\infty,0)} = f_1$ and $f|_{[0,\infty)} = f_2$.

So, the correct analogue of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:representables are sheaves} for continuous functions is the following.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:representables are sheaves}
    Let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces, and let $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ be an open cover of $X$. Then there is an equalizer diagram
    \[\Hom(X, Y) \to \prod_\alpha \Hom(U_\alpha, Y) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \Hom(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta, Y)\]
    in the category of sets. Here the two parallel arrows are defined by
    \[(f_\alpha)_\alpha \mapsto (f_\alpha |_{U_\alpha \cap U_\beta})_{\alpha,\beta}\]
    and
    \[(f_\alpha)_\alpha \mapsto (f_\beta |_{U_\alpha \cap U_\beta})_{\alpha,\beta}.\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Exercise.
\end{proof}

We note that the analogue of \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:sheaves are representable} does not hold in this context!

\begin{example}\label{example:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:non representable sheaf on Top}
    Let $q : [0,1] \to S^1$ be the standard quotient map. We say that a continuous function $g : X \to S^1$ is \emph{locally liftable} along $q$ if, for all $x \in X$, there exists an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ and a continuous function $\tilde{g} : U \to [0,1]$ such that $q \circ \tilde{g} = g|_U$.

    Let $\cal{F} : \Top^\op \to \Set$ be the subfunctor\footnote{This means simply that each $\cal{F}(X)$ is a subset of $\Hom(X,S^1)$, and that the inclusions $\cal{F}(X) \to \Hom(X,S^1)$ form a natural transformation (this determines what $\cal{F}$ does to morphisms). We'll talk more about subfunctors later!} of $\Hom({-},S^1)$ defined by
    \[\cal{F}(X) = \{g \in \Hom(X,S^1) : g~\text{is locally liftable along}~q\}.\]

    Then the following sheaf condition holds:
    \begin{description}
        \item[$(\star)$] For any space $X$ and any open cover $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ of $X$,
              \[\cal{F}(X) \to \prod_\alpha \cal{F}(U_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \cal{F}(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta)\]
              is an equalizer diagram in $\Set$.
    \end{description}
    However, $\cal{F}$ is not representable.
    \begin{proof}
        It is left as an exercise to show that $\cal{F}$ is a well-defined subfunctor of $\Hom({-},S^1)$ which satisfies the sheaf condition.

        Suppose for contradiction that $\cal{F}$ is representable, and pick a space $Y$ such that $\cal{F} \cong \Hom({-},Y)$. By the Yoneda lemma, this natural isomorphism is determined by an element $i \in \cal{F}(Y)$, i.e. a continuous function $i : Y \to S^1$ which is locally liftable along $q$. Precisely, the Yoneda lemma tells us that the natural isomorphism $\Hom({-},Y) \to \cal{F}$ is given by
        \begin{align*}
            \Hom(X,Y) & \to \cal{F}(X)     \\
            f         & \mapsto i \circ f.
        \end{align*}
        The map $i$ also gives us a natural transformation $\Hom({-},Y) \to \Hom({-},S^1)$ by postcomposition. For any space $X$, we see that the composite
        \[\cal{F}(X) \xrightarrow{\cong} \Hom(X,Y) \xrightarrow{i_*} \Hom(X,S^1)\]
        sends an element
        $g \in \cal{F}(X)$
        first to the unique morphism
        $f \in \Hom(X,Y)$
        such that $i \circ f = g$, and then to the morphism
        $i \circ f = g \in \Hom(X,S^1)$.
        In other words, this composite is the inclusion $\cal{F} \to \Hom({-},S^1)$. In particular, $i_* : \Hom(X,Y) \to \Hom(X,S^1)$ is injective for all spaces $X$, i.e.\ $i : Y \to S^1$ is a monomorphism\footnote{In $\Top$, monomorphisms are simply injective continuous functions.}.

        Now observe that $q$ is itself locally liftable along $q$, since $\id_{[0,1]}$ is a
        global lift. So $q \in \cal{F}([0,1])$, i.e.\ there is a unique continuous map
        $f : [0,1] \to Y$ with $i \circ f = q$. Since $q$ is a quotient map, $i$ is also a quotient map (in particular, $i$ is surjective). We have now established that $i$ is a bijective quotient map, i.e.\ a homeomorphism. Without loss of generality, $Y = S^1$ and $i = \id_{S^1}$. Then we have $\id_{S^1} = i \in \cal{F}(Y) = \cal{F}(S^1)$, which is a contradiction: $\id_{S^1}$ is not locally liftable along $q$ at the basepoint
        $\ast := q(0) = q(1)$.
    \end{proof}
\end{example}

\begin{remark*}[For experts! This remark can be safely skipped.]
    There's actually a lot going on in the background with this example. \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:sheaves are representable} is stated in a strange way. The more general fact is that for any site $(\catC,\tau)$, we have
    \[\Sh(\catC,\tau) \simeq \Sh(\Sh(\catC,\tau),\mathrm{can})\]
    via the Yoneda embedding. \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:intro:sheaves are representable} is an instantiation of this in the case that $\catC = \ast$ is the terminal category (via $\Sh(\ast) \simeq \Set$), but the statement there is a priori stronger, because we only posit the sheaf condition for literal coverings by subsets $X = \bigcup_{\alpha} X_\alpha$. This turns out to not make a difference, because the canonical topology on $\Set$ is generated by these coverings. So, for $\Top$, there are two reasons to expect an example like \Cref{example:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:non representable sheaf on Top}:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\Top$ is not a Grothendieck topos (this is the real sticking point).
        \item The canonical topology on $\Top$ is not generated by open covers.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{remark*}

We can alleviate a lot of this weirdness by focusing our attention on just one topological space, instead of the entire category $\Top$. First, some theory.

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:Open X}
    Let $X$ be a topological space. We denote by $\Open(X)$ the poset of open subsets of $X$, which we view as a category.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:presheaf}
    For any categories $\catC$ and $\catD$, a \emph{presheaf} on $\catC$ valued in $\catD$ is a functor $\cal{F} : \catC^\op \to \catD$. The category of presheaves will be denoted $\PSh(\catC,\catD) = \Fun(\catC^\op, \catD)$.

    A \emph{presheaf} on a space $X$ valued in $\catD$ is a functor $\cal{F} : \Open(X)^\op \to \catD$, i.e. $\PSh(X, \catD)$ is defined to be $\PSh(\Open(X), \catD)$.

    When $\catD$ is unspecified, it is assumed to be $\Set$. For example, when $X$ is a space, $\PSh(X)$ denotes the functor category $\Fun(\Open(X)^\op, \Set)$.
\end{definition}

We think of a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X,\catD)$ as a way of assigning, to each open subset $U \subseteq X$, a collection of data (an object of $\catD$) defined locally on $U$. As we have already seen, \emph{representable} presheaves are very important. These are intimately connected to the Yoneda lemma and its corollaries.

\begin{proposition}[Yoneda]\label{prop:Yoneda}
    Let $\catC$ be a small\footnote{For now, we will keep as a standing assumption that the domain of any presheaf is small.} category.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item There is a fully faithful functor $y : \catC \to \PSh(\catC)$ (the \emph{Yoneda embedding}) defined on objects by
              \[y(X) = \Hom_\catC({-},X)\]
              and acting on morphisms by postcomposition. Presheaves $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC)$ which lie in the essential image of $y$ (i.e. $\cal{F} \cong y(X)$ for some $X$) are called \emph{representable}.
        \item There is an isomorphism
              \[\Hom_{\PSh(\catC)}(y(X),\cal{F}) \cong \cal{F}(X)\]
              natural in both $X$ and $\cal{F}$. In one direction, a natural transformation $\alpha : y(X) \to \cal{F}$ determines an element $\alpha_X(\id_X) \in \cal{F}(X)$. In the other direction, an element $x \in \cal{F}(X)$ determines a natural transformation $\alpha : y(X) \to \cal{F}$ whose components are given by $\alpha_Y(f) = \cal{F}(f)(x)$.
        \item $y$ exhibits $\PSh(\catC)$ as the free cocompletion of $\catC$. That is: $\PSh(\catC)$ is cocomplete, and any functor $F : \catC \to \catD$ with $\catD$ cocomplete factors uniquely (up to natural isomorphism) as $G \circ y$ with $G : \PSh(\catC) \to \catD$ cocontinuous.
        \item Every presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC)$ is a colimit (in a canonical way) of representable presheaves. Explicitly, let $\int \cal{F}$ (the \emph{category of elements} of $\cal{F}$) be the category whose objects are pairs $(X,x)$ where $X \in \catC$ and $x \in \cal{F}(X)$ -- morphisms $(X,x) \to (Y,y)$ in $\int \cal{F}$ are morphisms $f : X \to Y$ in $\catC$ such that $\cal{F}(f)(y) = x$. There is a projection $\pi : \int \cal{F} \to \catC$ given by $(X,x) \mapsto X$. Composing this projection with the Yoneda embedding gives a functor $y \circ \pi : \int \cal{F} \to \PSh(\catC)$. For each $(X,x) \in \int \cal{F}$, there is a natural transformation $(y \circ \pi)(X,x) = y(X) \to \cal{F}$ corresponding to the element $x \in \cal{F}(X)$. This collection of natural transformations exhibits $\cal{F}$ as the colimit of $y \circ \pi$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}\label{example:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:examples of presheaves}
    Here are some examples of presheaves on spaces.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Let $X$ and $Y$ be spaces. There is a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$ given by $\cal{F}(U) = \Hom_\Top(U,Y)$. This is the composition of the representable presheaf $y(Y) : \Top^\op \to \Set$ with the (usually non-full!) inclusion $\Open(X)^\op \subseteq \Top^\op$.
        \item Let $X$ be a space and let $Y$ be a set. There is a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$ given by $\cal{F}(U) = Y$ for all $U \in \Open(X)$. This is a constant functor.
        \item Let $X$ be a space and let $V \in \Open(X)$. There is a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$ given by
              \[\cal{F}(U) = \begin{cases} \pt & U \subseteq V, \\ \varnothing & U \not\subseteq V. \end{cases}\]
              This is the representable presheaf $y(V)$.
        \item Let $X$ be a smooth manifold, so that every open subset of $X$ is also a smooth manifold in a canonical way. There is a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$ given by $\cal{F}(U) = \Hom_{\Man}(U,\RR)$, where $\Man$ is the category of smooth manifolds. This is the composition of the representable presheaf $y(\RR) : \Man^\op \to \Set$ with the (usually non-full!) inclusion $\Open(X)^\op \subseteq \Man^\op$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{example}

There are many, many more examples, but we must focus on something to start! Now we can turn to the sheaf condition for presheaves on spaces. It is precisely the generalization of the condition in \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:representables are sheaves}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf of sets}
    Let $X$ be a space. A presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$ is a \emph{sheaf} if for any open set $U \subseteq X$ and any open cover $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ of $U$, the diagram
    \[\cal{F}(U) \to \prod_\alpha \cal{F}(U_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \cal{F}(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta)\]
    is an equalizer diagram in $\Set$. The category of sheaves on $X$ (the full subcategory of $\PSh(X)$ spanned by all sheaves) will be denoted $\Sh(X)$.
\end{definition}

Defining sheaves with values in a generic category $\catD$ is quite subtle, philosophically. For now, we will handle only the case of ``algebraic categories'', by which I mean $\Set$, $\Grp$, $\Ab$, $\Ring$, and $\CRing$. In this case, the same definition makes sense, and is ``correct''.

\begin{warning}
    We will carry this definition of ``algebraic category'' throughout this section of the notes. It is extremely nonstandard and should not be taken seriously. The point is just that these particular categories $\Set$, $\Grp$, $\Ab$, $\Ring$, $\CRing$ are very well-behaved \emph{and} it is very important to consider sheaves with values in these categories.
\end{warning}

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf with values in algebraic category}
    Let $X$ be a space and let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. A presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X,\catD)$ is a \emph{sheaf} if for any open set $U \subseteq X$ and any open cover $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ of $U$, the diagram
    \[\cal{F}(U) \to \prod_\alpha \cal{F}(U_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \cal{F}(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta)\]
    is an equalizer diagram in $\catD$. The category of sheaves on $X$ with values in $\catD$ (the full subcategory of $\PSh(X,\catD)$ spanned by all sheaves) will be denoted $\Sh(X,\catD)$.
\end{definition}

Note that, for our algebraic categories (or indeed any category $\catD$ which is monadic over $\Set$), the forgetful functor $\catD \to \Set$ preserves and creates limits, so a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X,\catD)$ is a sheaf if and only if the underlying presheaf of sets is a sheaf. We now turn to the most important example of a sheaf on a space.

\begin{example}\label{example:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf of sections}
    Let $\pi : E \to X$ be a continuous function. There is a sheaf $\cal{S}_\pi \in \Sh(X)$ defined on objects by
    \[\cal{S}_\pi(U) = \{s \in \Hom_\Top(U,E) : \pi(s(x)) = x \text{ for all } x \in U\}\]
    and on morphisms by
    \begin{align*}
        \cal{S}_\pi(U \subseteq V) : \cal{S}_\pi(V) & \to \cal{S}_\pi(U) \\
        s                                           & \mapsto s|_U.
    \end{align*}
    This is called the \emph{sheaf of sections} of $\pi$.
\end{example}

One of the central theorems of sheaf theory is that every sheaf on a space arises as the sheaf of sections of some continuous function. We will prove this later -- for now, we note that this informs some common terminology.

\begin{notation*}
    When $\cal{F}$ is a (pre)sheaf on $X$ and $U$ is an open subset of $X$, we call the elements of $\cal{F}(U)$ \emph{local sections} of $\cal{F}$ over $U$. When $U = X$, we call the elements of $\cal{F}(X)$ \emph{global sections} of $\cal{F}$. When $U \subseteq V$ is an inclusion of open sets, the map
    \[\cal{F}(U \subseteq V) : \cal{F}(V) \to \cal{F}(U)\]
    is called \emph{restriction}, and may be denoted $\res^V_U$. We may even write $s|_U$ for $\res^V_U(s)$ when $s \in \cal{F}(V)$, even when $s$ is not literally a function!
\end{notation*}

\subsection{Operations and Functors on (Pre-)Sheaves}

Whenever $\catD$ is (co)complete, i.e.\ admits all small (co)limits, the category $\PSh(\catC,\catD)$ is also (co)complete, and these (co)limits are computed pointwise. In particular, for a diagram $F : \cal{I} \to \PSh(\catC,\catD)$ (with $\cal{I}$ a small category), we have
\[(\lim F)(c) \cong \lim_{i \in \cal{I}} F(i)(c) \qquad (\colim F)(c) \cong \colim_{i \in \cal{I}} F(i)(c)\]
for all objects $c \in \catC$. In particular, we may form products, coproducts, pullbacks, equalizers, etc.\ of presheaves quite easily. The same is true for \emph{limits} of sheaves on spaces\footnote{Actually, $\Sh(X)$ is both complete and cocomplete, but the colimits are not computed pointwise. We'll get into this later.}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves are complete}
    Let $X$ be a space and let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. Then $\Sh(X,\catD)$ is complete, and limits in $\Sh(X,\catD)$ are computed pointwise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Exercise.
\end{proof}

This tells us, for example, that morphisms of sheaves of abelian groups have kernels, which are computed pointwise, i.e.\ $\ker(\varphi)(U) = \ker(\varphi_U : \cal{F}(U) \to \cal{F}'(U))$ for any morphism $\varphi : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}'$ of sheaves of abelian groups and any open set $U \subseteq X$.

There are also constructions that allow us to move between different categories of (pre)sheaves.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:image of presheaves and adjoints}
    Let $F : \catC \to \catC'$ be any functor, and let $\catD$ be any category. There is a functor $F^* : \PSh(\catC', \catD) \to \PSh(\catC, \catD)$ given by precomposition with $F^\op$.

    Now assume moreover that $\catC$ and $\catC'$ are small.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item If $\catD$ is cocomplete, then $F^*$ admits a left adjoint $F_! : \PSh(\catC, \catD) \to \PSh(\catC', \catD)$ defined by
              \[(F_! \cal{F})(c') = \colim_{(c,u) \in (c' \downarrow F)^\op} \cal{F}(c).\]
        \item If $\catD$ is complete, then $F^*$ admits a right adjoint $F_* : \PSh(\catC, \catD) \to \PSh(\catC', \catD)$ defined by
              \[(F_* \cal{F})(c') = \lim_{(c,u) \in (F \downarrow c')^\op} \cal{F}(c).\]
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Exercise for experts.
\end{proof}

This proposition is stated in large generality so that we can use it in other contexts later -- it is not necessary to fully understand the definitions of $F_!$ and $F_*$; we only need to know they exist\footnote{Also, $F_*$ almost never appears in practice.}. For now, we only need the functors $F^*$ and $F_!$ in the special case that $F = f^{-1} : \Open(Y) \to \Open(X)$, where $f : X \to Y$ is a continuous map between spaces.

As a special case, we may consider the inclusion of a point $x \in X$, which we will denote $i_x : \pt \to X$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:stalk}
    Let $\catD$ be a category admitting all filtered colimits. For any space $X$ and any point $x \in X$, we define the \emph{stalk} of a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X,\catD)$ at $x$ to be the global sections of the presheaf $(i_x)^{-1}_\pre(\cal{F})$, i.e.
    \[\cal{F}_x := (i_x)^{-1}_\pre (\cal{F})(\pt) = \colim_{U \ni x} \cal{F}(U).\]
    This yields the \emph{stalk functor} $\cal{F} \mapsto \cal{F}_x :\PSh(X,\catD) \to \catD$.
\end{definition}

Stalks are very important invariants of a (pre)sheaf. In nice cases, filtered colimits in $\catD$ commute with finite limits, so the stalk functor preserves finite limits. We record this briefly since it is often useful.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:stalk functor preserves finite limits}
    Let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. Then $f^{-1}_\pre : \PSh(Y,\catD) \to \PSh(X,\catD)$ preserves finite limits for any continuous map $f$, and in particular the stalk functor $\cal{F} \mapsto \cal{F}_x : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \catD$ preserves finite limits for any point $x \in X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Abstract nonsense.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:germ}
    If $U \subseteq X$ is an open set containing a point $x$, then $U$ is one of the open sets indexing the colimit defining the stalk $\cal{F}_x$, and so we have a canonical map
    \[\cal{F}(U) \to \cal{F}_x\]
    which is natural in $\cal{F}$.

    When $\catD$ is one of our algebraic categories\footnote{Note that all of our algebraic categories are concrete, i.e. objects in $\catD$ have underlying sets and therefore elements.}, we write $s \mapsto s_x$ for the action of this map. We call $s_x$ the \emph{germ}\footnote{All of the terminology here is agricultural. A sheaf is a collected bundle of stalks of (e.g.) wheat, and each stalk of wheat has many germs (basically, the seeds/grains at the end of the stalk).} of the section $s$ at $x$.
\end{definition}

Intuitively, if we think of $s$ as some kind of function locally defined on $U$, its germ $s_x$ remembers only the behavior of $s$ at points ``arbitrarily close'' to $x$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:same germ means same locally}
    Let $s, t \in \cal{F}(U)$ be local sections of a presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X,\catD)$, where $\catD$ is algebraic. Let $x \in U$. We have $s_x = t_x$ in $\cal{F}_x$ if and only if there is some open neighborhood $V$ of $x$ such that $V \subseteq U$ and $s|_V = t|_V$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Exercise (this just boils down to how colimits are constructed in our algebraic categories).
\end{proof}

In fact, the above proposition can be generalized: we can take $s \in \cal{F}(U)$ and $t \in \cal{F}(U')$ for any two open sets $U, U' \subseteq X$ containing $x$, and find that $s_x = t_x$ if and only if there is some open neighborhood $V \subseteq U \cap U'$ of $x$ such that $s|_V = t|_V$. In particular, note that $(s|_V)_x = s_x$ whenever $x \in V \subseteq U$ and $s \in \cal{F}(U)$.

We finish with an omnibus theorem collecting some important facts about sheaves on spaces.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:facts about sheaves}
    Let $\catD$ be an algebraic category and $X, Y$ topological spaces. Then:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item For any continuous function $f : X \to Y$, the functor $f_* : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(Y,\catD)$ restricts to a functor $f_* : \Sh(X,\catD) \to \Sh(Y,\catD)$.
        \item For any point $x \in X$, the functor $(i_x)^{-1}_{\pre} : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(\pt, \catD)$ restricts to a functor $(i_x)^{-1}_{\pre} : \Sh(X,\catD) \to \Sh(\pt, \catD)$. Noting that taking global sections is an equivalence $\Sh(\pt,\catD) \to \catD$, we can view $(i_x)^{-1}_\pre$ as the stalk functor $\cal{F} \mapsto \cal{F}_x$.
        \item For any open set $U \subseteq X$ and any sheaf $\cal{F} \in \Sh(X,\catD)$, the morphism $\cal{F}(U) \to \prod_{x \in U} \cal{F}_x$ given by taking germs is injective.
        \item Let $\varphi : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}'$ be a morphism in $\Sh(X,\catD)$. Then $\varphi$ is an isomorphism if and only if the induced morphism on stalks $\varphi_x : \cal{F}_x \to \cal{F}'_x$ is an isomorphism for all $x \in X$.
        \item Let $\varphi_1, \varphi_2 : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}'$ be morphisms in $\Sh(X,\catD)$. We have $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2$ if and only if $(\varphi_1)_x = (\varphi_2)_x$ for all $x \in X$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:terminal sheaf detected by stalks}
    A sheaf $\cal{F}$ is terminal (in the category $\Sh(X,\catD)$) if and only if $\cal{F}_x$ is terminal in $\catD$ for all $x \in X$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    First, note that the terminal presheaf $\ast$ (the constant functor whose value is the terminal object in $\catD$) is also the terminal object of $\Sh(X,\catD)$, by \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves are complete}. We may compute directly that $\ast_x$ is terminal in $\catD$ for all $x \in X$. Then let $\cal{F} \in \Sh(X,\catD)$ be arbitrary. There is a unique morphism of sheaves $\cal{F} \to \ast$, which is an isomorphism if and only if $\cal{F}$ is terminal. By \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:facts about sheaves}, we conclude that $\cal{F}$ is terminal if and only if $\cal{F}_x \to \ast_x$ is an isomorphism for all $x \in X$. Since $\ast_x$ is terminal in $\catD$, this is equivalent to $\cal{F}_x$ being terminal in $\catD$ for all $x \in X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{slogan}\label{slogan:sheaves are governed by their stalks}
    Sheaves are governed by their stalks.
\end{slogan}

\begin{remark}\label{remark:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:why algebraic categories}
    This is a good time to return to our definition of ``algebraic category''. One may wonder why we don't simply define $\Sh(X,\catD)$ for $\catD$ an arbitrary category -- our \Cref{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf with values in algebraic category} makes perfect sense in general\footnote{Even if $\catD$ does not admit all equalizers, we can simply demand that each required equalizer does exist.}. There are two reasons we don't do this.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item In order to define stalks, we need $\catD$ to admit all filtered colimits.
        \item Even if $\catD$ is complete and cocomplete, so that we may define stalks and all of our nice constructions, \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:facts about sheaves}(iv) (and thus \Cref{slogan:sheaves are governed by their stalks}) may fail. See \Cref{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves of spaces are bad} for an example.
    \end{itemize}
    Really, the only category of sheaves on $X$ that matters is $\Sh(X,\Set)$ -- sheaves with values in other categories are correctly interpreted ``internally'', i.e.\ as sheaves of sets with extra structure (see \Cref{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:ShAb is AbSh}). For our algebraic categories, it turns out that it is equivalent to take the ``external'' definition of sheaves with values in $\catD$ or the ``internal'' definition of sheaves of sets with extra structure (and often convenient to define things in the external way).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Open Embeddings}

We have seen (in \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:facts about sheaves}) that for any continuous map $f : X \to Y$, the functor $f_* : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(Y,\catD)$ preserves sheaves. Its left adjoint $f^{-1}_\pre$, however, does not preserve sheaves in general (see \Cref{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:preimage does not preserve sheaves}). If $f$ is an open embedding, this problem goes away.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:open embedding preimage preserves sheaves}
    Let $i : U \to X$ be an open embedding\footnote{In other words, up to homeomorphism, $i$ is the inclusion of an open subspace of $X$.}. Then the functor $i^{-1}_\pre : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(U,\catD)$ preserves sheaves, and thus restricts to a functor $i^{-1}_\pre : \Sh(X,\catD) \to \Sh(U,\catD)$ which is left adjoint to $i_* : \Sh(U,\catD) \to \Sh(X,\catD)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    We may compute directly that
    \[(i^{-1}_\pre \cal{F})(V) = \colim_{W \supseteq i(V)} \cal{F}(W) = \cal{F}(i(V))\]
    for any open set $V \subseteq U$. Then the sheaf condition for $\cal{F}$ on $X$ implies the sheaf condition for $i^{-1}_\pre \cal{F}$ on $U$. The adjunction follows from the fact that $\Sh(X,\catD)$ is a full subcategory of $\PSh(X,\catD)$.
\end{proof}

In the case that $i : U \to X$ is literally the inclusion of an open subspace (as opposed to an arbitrary open embedding), we often write $\cal{F} \mapsto \cal{F}|_U$ for the action of the functor $i^{-1}_\pre$, and we call $\cal{F}|_U$ the \emph{restriction} of $\cal{F}$ to $U$.

The fact that $i^{-1}_\pre : \Sh(X,\catD) \to \Sh(U,\catD)$ is well-defined when $i$ is an open embedding is explained by an underlying relationship between $\Open(U)$ and $\Open(X)$, which we will now explore.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:adjunction between Open U and Open X}
    Let $i : U \to X$ be an open embedding. Then the functor $i^{-1} : \Open(X) \to \Open(U)$ is right adjoint to the functor $i : \Open(U) \to \Open(X)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Elementary.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{remark:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:open maps and adjunctions}
    In fact, for any open map $f : X \to Y$, the functor $f^{-1} : \Open(Y) \to \Open(X)$ is right adjoint to the functor $f : \Open(X) \to \Open(Y)$. So, the work in this subsection can be generalized to this more general situation.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:adjunction between presheaves induced by adjunction between categories}
    Let $\catC$ and $\catC'$ be small categories, and let $\catD$ be any category. Let

            \catC \\
            {\catC'}
            \arrow[""{name=0, anchor=center, inner sep=0}, "F"', shift right=2, from=1-1, to=2-1]

    be an adjunction ($F$ is the left adjoint). Then the functor $F^* : \PSh(\catC', \catD) \to \PSh(\catC, \catD)$ is left adjoint to the functor $G^* : \PSh(\catC, \catD) \to \PSh(\catC', \catD)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    The adjunction $F \dashv G$ is determined by a unit $\eta : \id_\catC \to G \circ F$ and a counit $\varepsilon : F \circ G \to \id_{\catC'}$. The adjunction $F^* \dashv G^*$ has unit
    \[\id_{\PSh(\catC', \catD)} = \id_{\catC'}^* \to (F \circ G)^* = G^* \circ F^*\]
    given by precomposition with $\varepsilon$, and counit
    \[F^* \circ G^* = (G \circ F)^* \to \id_\catC^* = \id_{\PSh(\catC, \catD)}\]
    given by precomposition with $\eta$. We leave the verification of the triangle identities as an exercise.
\end{proof}

I apologize for the confusing overloading of notation: here $i$ denotes both the map of spaces $U \to X$ as well as the map of posets $\Open(U) \to \Open(X)$. In the notation of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:image of presheaves and adjoints}, we have (by definition)
\[i^{-1}_\pre : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(U,\catD) \quad = \quad (i^{-1} : \Open(X) \to \Open(U))_!\]
and
\[i_* : \PSh(U,\catD) \to \PSh(X,\catD) \quad = \quad (i^{-1} : \Open(X) \to \Open(U))^*.\]
Now the adjunction $i \dashv i^{-1}$ of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:adjunction between Open U and Open X} gives us an adjunction $i^* \dashv (i^{-1})^*$ by \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:adjunction between presheaves induced by adjunction between categories}, while we also have the adjunction $(i^{-1})_! \dashv (i^{-1})^*$ from \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:image of presheaves and adjoints}. By uniqueness of adjoints, we conclude that $(i^{-1})_! \cong i^*$. This makes it almost immediate that $i^{-1}_\pre$ preserves sheaves, because it is given by $i^*$ and $i : \Open(U) \to \Open(X)$ sends open covers to open covers\footnote{This is the same reason that $f_* = (f^{-1})^*$ preserves sheaves for any continuous map $f$.}.

We get even more: $i^*$ admits a left adjoint $i_!$, extending our adjoint pair $i^{-1}_\pre \dashv i_*$ to an adjoint triple
\[\underbrace{i_!}_{i_!} \dashv \underbrace{i^* = (i^{-1})_!}_{i^{-1}_\pre} \dashv \underbrace{i_* = (i^{-1})^*}_{i_*}\]
of functors between categories of presheaves. The underbraces indicate the standard notation for each functor, grouping together various functors written in the notation of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:image of presheaves and adjoints}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:extension by zero}
    Let $\catD$ be a cocomplete category. The functor \[i_! : \PSh(U,\catD) \to \PSh(X,\catD)\] is given on objects by
    \[(i_! \cal{F})(V) = \begin{cases}
            \cal{F}(V) & : V \subseteq U     \\
            0          & : V \not\subseteq U
        \end{cases}\]
    where $0$ denotes the initial object of $\catD$. The operation $i_!$ is called \emph{extension by zero}. If $\catD = \Set$, then $i_!$ preserves sheaves.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    From \Cref{cor:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:image of presheaves on spaces and adjoints}, we have
    \[(i_! \cal{F})(V) = \colim_{i(W) \supseteq V} \cal{F}(W) = \begin{cases}
            \cal{F}(V) & : V \subseteq U     \\
            0          & : V \not\subseteq U
        \end{cases}.\]
    If $\catD = \Set$ then $0 = \varnothing$, and the sheaf condition for $i_! \cal{F}$ follows from the sheaf condition for $\cal{F}$ together with the fact that if $V \not\subseteq U$, then any open cover of $V$ must contain some open set $W \not\subseteq U$.
\end{proof}

The restriction functor $\cal{F} \mapsto \cal{F}|_U$ also allows us to define an enrichment of the category of (pre)sheaves.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:enrichment of presheaves}
    Let $X$ be a space and $\catD$ a category enriched over a base $\cal{V}$ which is complete, cocomplete, and closed symmetric monoidal.

    First, the category $\PSh(X,\catD)$ is naturally enriched over $\cal{V}$, with
    \[\Hom(\cal{F}, \cal{F}') := \int_{U \in \Open(X)^\op} \Hom(\cal{F}(U), \cal{F}'(U)).\]

    Moreover, $\PSh(X,\catD)$ is naturally enriched over $\PSh(X,\cal{V})$, with
    \[\underline{\Hom}(\cal{F}, \cal{F}')(U) := \Hom_{\PSh(U,\catD)}(\cal{F}|_U, \cal{F}'|_U)\]
    for each open set $U \subseteq X$. Note that the global sections of $\underline{\Hom}(\cal{F}, \cal{F}')$ are precisely the enriched hom $\Hom(\cal{F}, \cal{F}') \in \cal{V}$.

    If $\catD$ and $\cal{V}$ are moreover algebraic\footnote{The only options for $\cal{V}$ here are $\Set$ and $\Ab$.}, then the same holds for sheaves: $\Sh(X,\catD)$ is enriched over $\cal{V}$ and over $\Sh(X,\cal{V})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    It is clear that $\underline{\Hom}(\cal{F}, \cal{F}')$ is a well-defined presheaf on $X$ when $\cal{F}$ and $\cal{F}'$ are presheaves\footnote{Make sure you know what the restriction maps are!}. We leave it as an exercise to show that $\underline{\Hom}(\cal{F}, \cal{F}')$ is a sheaf whenever $\cal{F}'$ is a sheaf.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf determined by restriction to basis}
    Let $X$ be a space and let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. Let $\cal{B}$ be a basis\footnote{Recall that a basis $\cal{B}$ for the topology on $X$ is a collection of open subsets of $X$ such that every open subset $U$ of $X$ can be written as a union $\bigcup \cal{V}$ for some $\cal{V} \subseteq \cal{B}$.} for the topology on $X$. We may view $\cal{B}$ as a full subcategory of $\Open(X)$. Then the restriction functor $\Sh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(\cal{B},\catD)$ is fully faithful.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Exercise.
\end{proof}

We note that this does not hold for presheaves on $X$! In part, this proposition says that a sheaf is determined by its values on a basis of the topology -- this requires the sheaf condition. It's also important to note that this restriction functor is not essentially surjective, i.e. not every presheaf on a basis of the topology extends to a sheaf on the whole space.

We can characterize exactly which presheaves on $\cal{B}$ come from sheaves on $X$. It is \emph{almost} enough to simply say that they are the presheaves on $\cal{B}$ which ``satisfy the sheaf condition'' -- the problem is that $\cal{B}$ need not be closed under intersection, so terms like $\cal{F}(B_1 \cap B_2)$ naturally appearing in a naive version of the sheaf condition are not well-defined. However, since $\cal{B}$ is a basis, any intersection $B_1 \cap B_2$ can itself be covered by elements of $\cal{B}$!

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf on a basis}
    Let $X$ be a space, let $\cal{B}$ be a basis for the topology on $X$, and let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. A presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\cal{B},\catD)$ is a \emph{sheaf on $\cal{B}$} if the following  sheaf condition holds:
    \begin{description}
        \item[$(\star)$] For every $B \in \cal{B}$, every open cover $B = \bigcup_\alpha B_\alpha$ with each $B_\alpha \in \cal{B}$, and every collection of open covers\footnote{That is, we choose an open cover of $B_\alpha \cap B_\beta$. The indexing set of $\gamma$ therefore depends on $\alpha$ and $\beta$.}
              \[B_\alpha \cap B_\beta = \bigcup_\gamma B_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma}\]
              with each $B_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma} \in \cal{B}$, the diagram
              \[\cal{F}(B) \to \prod_\alpha \cal{F}(B_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \prod_\gamma \cal{F}(B_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma})\]
              is an equalizer diagram in $\catD$.
    \end{description}
    We write $\Sh(\cal{B},\catD)$ for the full subcategory of $\PSh(\cal{B},\catD)$ spanned by the sheaves.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{remark:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:basis closed under intersections}
    Suppose moreover that $\cal{B}$ is closed under finite intersections. Then $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\cal{B},\catD)$ is a sheaf if and only if, for every $B \in \cal{B}$ and every cover $B = \bigcup_\alpha B_\alpha$ by elements of $\cal{B}$, the diagram
    \[\cal{F}(B) \to \prod_\alpha \cal{F}(B_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \cal{F}(B_\alpha \cap B_\beta)\]
    is an equalizer diagram in $\catD$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves on a basis}
    Restriction along the inclusion $\cal{B} \subseteq \Open(X)$ is an equivalence of categories
    \[\Sh(X,\catD) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \Sh(\cal{B},\catD).\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    The inverse is given by right Kan extension along the inclusion $\cal{B} \to \Open(X)$: for $\cal{F} \in \Sh(\cal{B},\catD)$ and $U \in \Open(X)$, set
    \[\widetilde{\cal{F}}(U) := \lim_{B \subseteq U} \cal{F}(B).\]
    Then\footnote{Exercise!} $\widetilde{\cal{F}}$ is a sheaf on $X$, and the restriction of $\widetilde{\cal{F}}$ to $\cal{B}$ is (naturally isomorphic to) $\cal{F}$.
\end{proof}

The intuitive idea in the proof above is that \emph{any} open subset $U$ of $X$ may be covered by elements $B_\alpha$ of $\cal{B}$, and the intersections $B_\alpha \cap B_\beta$ may again be covered by elements of $\cal{B}$, allowing us to define $\widetilde{\cal{F}}(U)$ as the equalizer of
\[\prod_\alpha \cal{F}(B_\alpha) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha, \beta} \prod_\gamma \cal{F}(B_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma}).\]
The only issue with this construction is that then the object $\widetilde{\cal{F}}(U)$ is only defined up to isomorphism, since each $U$ may admit many distinct covers $\{B_\alpha\}_\alpha$, and each intersection $B_\alpha \cap B_\beta$ may admit many distinct covers $\{B_{\alpha, \beta, \gamma}\}_\gamma$. The easy fix is to choose a specific canonical cover of $U$ by elements of $\cal{B}$, namely $U = \bigcup \{B \in \cal{B} : B \subseteq U\}$, and do likewise for the intersections. This is exactly what the Kan extension does for us.

This theorem is a very useful tool for constructing sheaves in practice. For example, this is by far the easiest way to construct the structure sheaf of an affine scheme\footnote{We'll talk about this later in the notes when we introduce schemes.}. The structure sheaf on an arbitrary scheme is then ``glued'' together from the structure sheaves of its affine open subschemes, so it is also worth discussing how we may glue sheaves together.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing sheaves}
    Let $X$ be a space, let $\catD$ be an algebraic category, and let $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ be an open cover of $X$. Write $U_{\alpha,\beta} := U_\alpha \cap U_\beta$ and $U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma} := U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \cap U_\gamma$. Suppose we are given:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item For each $\alpha$, a sheaf $\cal{F}_\alpha \in \Sh(U_\alpha,\catD)$, and
        \item For each pair $\alpha,\beta$, an isomorphism
              \[\varphi_{\alpha,\beta} : \cal{F}_\beta|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}} \xrightarrow{\cong} \cal{F}_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}\]
              in $\Sh(U_{\alpha,\beta},\catD)$,
    \end{enumerate}
    satisfying the \emph{cocycle condition}: for all $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$,
    \[\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}|_{U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}} \circ \varphi_{\beta,\gamma}|_{U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}} = \varphi_{\alpha,\gamma}|_{U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}\]
    as morphisms $\cal{F}_\gamma|_{U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}} \to \cal{F}_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}$.

    Then there is a sheaf $\cal{F} \in \Sh(X,\catD)$, together with isomorphisms
    \[\psi_\alpha : \cal{F}|_{U_\alpha} \xrightarrow{\cong} \cal{F}_\alpha\]
    in $\Sh(U_\alpha,\catD)$, such that
    \[\varphi_{\alpha,\beta} \circ \left(\psi_\beta|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}\right) = \psi_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}\]
    for all $\alpha,\beta$. Moreover, the data $\left(\cal{F},(\psi_\alpha)\right)$ is unique up to unique isomorphism (in the appropriate sense).
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}\label{remark:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:cocycle condition implies normalized}
    It follows from the cocycle condition\footnote{Exercise!} that $\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha} = \id_{\cal{F}_\alpha}$ for all $\alpha$, and that $\varphi_{\beta,\alpha} = \varphi_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}$ for all $\alpha,\beta$.
\end{remark}
\begin{proof}
    Define $\cal{F}$ to be the equalizer of
    \[\prod_\alpha (i_\alpha)_* \cal{F}_\alpha \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha,\beta} (i_{\alpha,\beta})_* \cal{F}_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}\]
    where $i_\alpha : U_\alpha \to X$ and $i_{\alpha,\beta} : U_{\alpha,\beta} \to X$ are the inclusions, and the two maps are given on local sections by
    \begin{align*}
        f_V, g_V : \prod_\alpha \cal{F}_\alpha(V \cap U_\alpha) & \to \prod_{\alpha,\beta} \cal{F}_\alpha(V \cap U_{\alpha,\beta})                                                 \\
        f_V = (s_\alpha)_\alpha                                 & \mapsto (s_\alpha|_{V \cap U_{\alpha,\beta}})_{\alpha,\beta},                                                    \\
        g_V = (s_\alpha)_\alpha                                 & \mapsto ((\varphi_{\alpha,\beta})_{V \cap U_{\alpha,\beta}}(s_\beta|_{V \cap U_{\alpha,\beta}}))_{\alpha,\beta}.
    \end{align*}
    Note that $(\alpha,\beta)$-component of $f$ is simply the projection
    \[\prod_\gamma (i_\gamma)_* \cal{F}_\gamma \to (i_\alpha)_* \cal{F}_\alpha\]
    onto the $\alpha$-component followed by $(i_\alpha)_*$ applied to the unit
    \[\cal{F}_\alpha \to j_* j^{-1}_\pre \cal{F}_\alpha,\]
    of the adjunction $j^{-1}_\pre \dashv j_*$, where $j : U_{\alpha,\beta} \to U_\alpha$ is the inclusion. Similarly, the $(\alpha,\beta)$-component of $g$ is the projection onto the $\beta$-component, followed by $(i_\beta)_*$ applied to the unit $\cal{F}_\beta \to k_* k^{-1}_\pre \cal{F}_\beta$ (where $k : U_{\alpha,\beta} \to U_\beta$ is the inclusion), followed by $(i_{\alpha,\beta})_*$ applied to $\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}$. Thus, $f$ and $g$ are morphisms of presheaves, and $\cal{F}$ is a well-defined sheaf.

    Note that, when $V \subseteq U_\alpha$, any section $(s_\beta)_\beta \in \cal{F}(V)$ satisfies
    \begin{equation}\tag{\(\dagger\)}\label{eq:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing sheaves:compatibility of sections}
        s_\beta = (\varphi_{\beta,\alpha})_{V \cap U_\beta}(s_\alpha|_{V \cap U_\beta})
    \end{equation}
    for all $\beta$, by considering the $(\beta,\alpha)$-component of the equality $f_V((s_\beta)_\beta) = g_V((s_\beta)_\beta)$. We define\footnote{More abstractly: $\cal{F}$ comes equipped with maps $\cal{F} \to (i_\alpha)_* \cal{F}_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$. By the adjunction $(i_\alpha)^{-1}_\pre \dashv (i_\alpha)_*$, these correspond to maps $\cal{F}|_{U_\alpha} \to \cal{F}_\alpha$, which are the $\psi_\alpha$'s.} $\psi_\alpha : \cal{F}|_{U_\alpha} \to \cal{F}_\alpha$ by $(\psi_\alpha)_V = (s_\beta)_\beta \mapsto s_\alpha$ for all $V \in \Open(U_\alpha)$, and note that we have just shown that $\psi_\alpha$ is injective.

    To show that $\psi_\alpha$ is surjective, we let $V \subseteq U_\alpha$ be an arbitrary open set and $t \in \cal{F}_\alpha(V)$ an arbitrary section. Then define \[s_\beta := (\varphi_{\beta,\alpha})_{V \cap U_\beta}(t|_{V \cap U_\beta}) \in \cal{F}_\beta(V \cap U_\beta)\] for each $\beta$. First, we claim that $(s_\beta)_\beta \in \cal{F}(V)$. Indeed, we have
    \begin{multline*}
        f_V((s_\beta)_\beta)
        = (s_\beta|_{V \cap U_{\beta,\gamma}})_{\beta, \gamma}
        = ((\varphi_{\beta,\alpha})_{V \cap U_\beta}(t|_{V \cap U_\beta})|_{V \cap U_{\beta,\gamma}})_{\beta, \gamma} \\
        = ((\varphi_{\beta,\alpha})_{V \cap U_{\beta,\gamma}}(t|_{V \cap U_{\beta,\gamma}}))_{\beta, \gamma}
    \end{multline*}
    and also
    \begin{multline*}
        g_V((s_\beta)_\beta)
        = ((\varphi_{\beta,\gamma})_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}}(s_\gamma|_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}}))_{\beta, \gamma} \\
        = ((\varphi_{\beta,\gamma})_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}}((\varphi_{\gamma,\alpha})_{V \cap U_\gamma}(t|_{V \cap U_\gamma})|_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}}))_{\beta, \gamma} \\
        = ((\varphi_{\beta,\gamma})_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}}((\varphi_{\gamma,\alpha})_{V \cap U_{\beta,\gamma}}(t|_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}})))_{\beta, \gamma} \\
        = ((\varphi_{\beta,\alpha})_{V \cap U_{\beta,\gamma}}(t|_{V \cap U_{\beta, \gamma}}))_{\beta, \gamma},
    \end{multline*}
    with the last equality coming from the cocycle condition. This shows that $(s_\beta)_\beta \in \cal{F}(V)$, and we have
    \[(\psi_\alpha)_V((s_\beta)_\beta) = s_\alpha = (\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha})_{V \cap U_\alpha}(t|_{V \cap U_\alpha}) = (\varphi_{\alpha,\alpha})_V(t) = t.\]
    The last equality here comes again from the cocycle condition. Thus $(\psi_\alpha)_V((s_\beta)_\beta) = t$, and we have shown that $(\psi_\alpha)_V$ is surjective. This shows that $\psi_\alpha$ is an isomorphism. Next, we must show that $\varphi_{\alpha,\beta} \circ \left(\psi_\beta|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}\right) = \psi_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}$. This is a consequence of \eqref{eq:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing sheaves:compatibility of sections}: when $W \subseteq U_{\alpha,\beta}$ is open, any section $(s_\gamma)_\gamma \in \cal{F}(W)$ satisfies
    \begin{align*}
        (\psi_\alpha)_W (s_\gamma)_\gamma & = s_\alpha                                                              \\
                                          & = (\varphi_{\alpha,\beta})_{W \cap U_\beta}(s_\beta|_{W \cap U_\alpha}) \\
                                          & = (\varphi_{\alpha,\beta})_W(s_\beta)                                   \\
                                          & = (\varphi_{\alpha,\beta})_W ((\psi_\beta)_W(s_\gamma)_\gamma).
    \end{align*}
    Finally, we must establish the uniqueness of $(\cal{F}, (\psi_\alpha)_\alpha)$, up to unique isomorphism. Suppose $(\cal{F}', (\psi'_\alpha)_\alpha)$ is another such datum. Then the maps $\psi'_\alpha : \cal{F}'|_{U_\alpha} \to \cal{F}_\alpha$ correspond to maps $\cal{F}' \to (i_\alpha)_* \cal{F}_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$, giving us a map
    \[\cal{F}' \to \prod_\alpha (i_\alpha)_* \cal{F}_\alpha.\]
    The compatibility condition $\varphi_{\alpha,\beta} \circ \left(\psi'_\beta|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}\right) = \psi'_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha,\beta}}$ says exactly that this map factors through the equalizer $\cal{F}$. The morphism $\cal{F}' \to \cal{F}$ becomes an isomorphism after restriction to each $U_\alpha$, since
    \[\cal{F}_\alpha \xrightarrow{(\psi_\alpha')^{-1}} \cal{F}'|_{U_\alpha} \to \cal{F}|_{U_\alpha} \xrightarrow{\psi_\alpha} \cal{F}_\alpha\]
    is the identity by construction. We therefore obtain a unique isomorphism $\cal{F}' \xrightarrow{\cong} \cal{F}$ compatible with the $\psi_\alpha$'s and $\psi'_\alpha$'s, as desired.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    \label{cor:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing morphisms of sheaves}
    Let $X$ be a space, let $\catD$ be an algebraic category, and let $\cal{F}, \cal{F}' \in \Sh(X,\catD)$. Let $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ be an open cover of $X$, and suppose we are given morphisms
    \[\varphi_\alpha : \cal{F}|_{U_\alpha} \to \cal{F}'|_{U_\alpha}\]
    in $\Sh(U_\alpha,\catD)$ for each $\alpha$, such that
    \[\varphi_\alpha|_{U_{\alpha} \cap U_{\beta}} = \varphi_\beta|_{U_{\alpha} \cap U_{\beta}}\]
    for all $\alpha,\beta$. Then there is a unique morphism $\varphi : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}'$ in $\Sh(X,\catD)$ such that $\varphi|_{U_\alpha} = \varphi_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Apply \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing sheaves} to the sheaves $\cal{F}|_{U_\alpha}$ and $\cal{F}'|_{U_\alpha}$ to reconstruct $\cal{F}$ and $\cal{F}'$ (up to isomorphism) from their restrictions. The maps $\varphi_\alpha$ assemble into a morphism between these reconstructions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{remark:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:Sh is a stack}
    \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing sheaves} and \Cref{cor:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:gluing morphisms of sheaves} say, in a sense that can be made precise, that the functor
    \[\Sh(X,\catD) \to \lim\left(\prod_\alpha \Sh(U_\alpha,\catD) \rightrightarrows \prod_{\alpha,\beta} \Sh(U_{\alpha,\beta},\catD) \triplerightarrow \prod_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma} \Sh(U_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma},\catD)\right)\]
    is an equivalence, where the limit\footnote{Technically, a \emph{pseudo-limit}} is taken in the $2$-category of categories. In other words, $U \mapsto \Sh(U,\catD)$ is some sort of ``higher categorical'' sheaf\footnote{If you want a fancy word, $U \mapsto \Sh(U,\catD)$ is an example of a \emph{stack}.} on $X$.

    Notice that triple intersections have appeared here for the first time. For a sheaf of sets $\cal{F} \in \Sh(X)$, the agreement of two sections $s \in \cal{F}(U), s' \in \cal{F}(V)$ on a double intersection $U \cap V$ is a \emph{condition}; we either have $s|_{U \cap V} = s'|_{U \cap V}$ or not. For a ``sheaf of categories'' like $U \mapsto \Sh(U,\catD)$, the agreement of two sections $\cal{F} \in \Sh(U,\catD), \cal{F}' \in \Sh(V,\catD)$ on a double intersection is \emph{data} (a choice of isomorphism $\varphi : \cal{F}'|_{U \cap V} \to \cal{F}|_{U \cap V}$); if two sheaves on $U \cap V$ are isomorphic, they may be isomorphic in many different ways. We therefore need to enforce that these isomorphisms themselves agree on triple intersections (this is the cocycle condition). An agreement between two isomorphisms is just a condition, however: two isomorphisms are either equal or not. So, we can stop there, and we don't need to consider quadruple intersections. In general, you could imagine that a ``sheaf of $n$-categories'' would require agreement data specified up to $(n+1)$-fold intersections, and agreement conditions on $(n+2)$-fold intersections.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Espace Étalé}

We have had to be careful in checking when certain functors preserve sheaves. We've seen that, for $f : X \to Y$ a continuous function, $f_* : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(Y,\catD)$ always preserves sheaves, but $f^{-1}_\pre : \PSh(Y,\catD) \to \PSh(X,\catD)$ does not always preserve sheaves. where we would like to have a left adjoint to $f_* : \Sh(X,\catD) \to \Sh(Y,\catD)$, but we don't always know how to construct it -- it is certainly not as simple as just using $f^{-1}_\pre$! It may be tempting to think that $f_* : \Sh(X, \catD) \to \Sh(Y, \catD)$ simply does not have a left adjoint unless $f^{-1}_\pre$ happens to preserve sheaves.

On the other hand, we know that $f_* : \PSh(X,\catD) \to \PSh(Y,\catD)$ preserves limits (because it is a right adjoint), and we know that limits of sheaves are computed pointwise, so $f_* : \Sh(X,\catD) \to \Sh(Y,\catD)$ also preserves limits. So, it also seems possible that $f_* : \Sh(X, \catD) \to \Sh(Y, \catD)$ always has a left adjoint! This turns out to be the truth.

Actually, this desired left adjoint comes from the fact that the inclusion $\Sh(X,\catD) \hookrightarrow \PSh(X,\catD)$ has a left adjoint, called \emph{sheafification}. In this subsection, we will construct the sheafification functor -- once this is in place, the left adjoint to $f_* : \Sh(X, \catD) \to \Sh(Y, \catD)$ has a very simple formula:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item First, view a sheaf $\cal{F} \in \Sh(Y,\catD)$ as a presheaf.
    \item Then, apply $f^{-1}_\pre$ to get a presheaf $f^{-1}_\pre \cal{F} \in \PSh(X,\catD)$.
    \item Finally, apply the sheafification functor to get a sheaf on $X$.
\end{enumerate}

The most efficient way to construct the sheafification functor is to use the \emph{espace étalé}\footnote{This is French terminology. \emph{Espace} means ``space'' and \emph{étalé} means something like ``spread out''.} construction. This is a very intuitive construction, with only two downsides:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It only makes sense to apply directly to sheaves of sets. This is not such a big deal, since (as mentioned previously) we can always view a sheaf with values in $\catD$ (for $\catD$ any algebraic category) as a sheaf of sets with extra structure, and it will be easy to see that the sheafification procedure preserves this extra structure.
    \item It does not generalize very easily to sheaves on sites (which we'll discuss later in the course), or at least the generalization to sites is not as intuitive. We'll introduce a different construction of sheafification later in the course that works for sheaves on sites.
\end{itemize}

So, for the rest of this subsection, we will restrict our attention to the case $\catD = \Set$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:espace etale}
    Let $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$. The \emph{espace étalé} of $\cal{F}$ is the topological space $E_{\cal{F}}$ whose underlying set is the disjoint union of stalks
    \[E_{\cal{F}} := \coprod_{x \in X} \cal{F}_x\]
    and whose topology is generated by the sets
    \[s[U] := \{(x,s_x) : x \in U\} \subseteq E_{\cal{F}}\]
    for all open sets $U \subseteq X$ and all sections $s \in \cal{F}(U)$. Here we write elements of the disjoint union $\coprod_{x \in X} \cal{F}_x$ as pairs $(x,g)$ with $x \in X$ and $g \in \cal{F}_x$.

    The \emph{canonical projection} $\pi : E_{\cal{F}} \to X$ is defined by $\pi(x,g) := x$. It is left as an exercise to check that $\pi$ is continuous.
\end{definition}

We record some useful topological properties of the espace étalé, which should help with understanding what it is like.

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:local section of presheaf is local section of espace etale}
    Let $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$, and let $s \in \cal{F}(U)$ be a local section of $\cal{F}$. Then the map $f : U \to E_{\cal{F}}$ defined by $f(x) := (x,s_x)$ is continuous.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    To see that $f$ is continuous, it suffices to show that $f^{-1}(s'[U'])$ is open for all open sets $U' \subseteq X$ and all local sections $s' \in \cal{F}(U')$. By definition of $f$, we have
    \[
        f^{-1}(s'[U']) = \{x \in U \cap U' : s_x = s'_x\}.
    \]
    This is an open set by \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:same germ means same locally}.
\end{proof}

In other words, every local section of the presheaf $\cal{F}$ gives rise to a local section of the canonical projection $\pi : E_{\cal{F}} \to X$. It is worth making an explicit warning that the converse is not always true! The point is that local sections of $\cal{F}$ will correspond exactly to local sections of $\pi$ if and only if $\cal{F}$ is a sheaf.

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:espace etale is local homeo}
    The map $\pi$ is a \emph{local homeomorphism}, i.e.\ for every $(x,g) \in E_{\cal{F}}$, there is an open neighborhood $V \subseteq E_{\cal{F}}$ of $(x,g)$ such that \[\pi|_V : V \to \pi(V)\] is a homeomorphism and $\pi(V)$ is open in $X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Let $(x,g) \in E_{\cal{F}}$ be arbitrary. By definition, there is an open neighborhood $U \subseteq X$ of $x$ and a section $s \in \cal{F}(U)$ such that $s_x = g$. Then $s[U]$ is an open neighborhood of $(x,g)$ in $E_{\cal{F}}$, and we claim that $\pi|_{s[U]} : s[U] \to U$ is a homeomorphism. This is straightforward: it is clear that $\pi|_{s[U]}$ is bijective with inverse $y \mapsto (y,s_y)$, and this inverse is continuous by the previous proposition.
\end{proof}

Local homeomorphisms are a very nice class of maps, and can be viewed as a generalization of covering spaces. In particular, any covering space is a local homeomorphism.

\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    The fiber $\pi^{-1}(x)$ can be constructed by pullback:

The espace étalé $E_\cal{F}$ gives a canonical way to build a sheaf from a presheaf: first construct the espace étalé, and then take the sheaf of sections of the canonical projection. \begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:espace etale is functorial}
    The espace étalé construction $\cal{F} \mapsto E_{\cal{F}}$ is functorial $\PSh(X) \to \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Given a morphism of presheaves $\varphi : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}'$, we obtain morphisms of stalks $\varphi_x : \cal{F}_x \to \cal{F}'_x$, which together give a function $E_\varphi : E_{\cal{F}} \to E_{\cal{F}'}$ which commutes with the canonical projections. It is left as an exercise to show that $E_\varphi$ is continuous, etc.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaf of sections is functorial}
    The sheaf of sections construction $p \mapsto \cal{S}_p$ is functorial $\Top/X \to \Sh(X)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Given a morphism $\varphi : E \to E'$ between maps $p : E \to X, p' : E' \to X$, we may define a morphism of sheaves $\cal{S}_{\varphi} : \cal{S}_p \to \cal{S}_{p'}$ on local sections by $s \mapsto \varphi \circ s$. It is left as an exercise to show that $\cal{S}_{\varphi}$ is well-defined, etc.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    \label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:stalks of local homeo are fibers}
    Let $p : E \to X$ be a local homeomorphism, and let $x \in X$ be arbitrary. There is a canonical bijection $(\cal{S}_p)_x \to p^{-1}(x)$ given by $s_x \mapsto s(x)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Let $x \in X$ and $t \in p^{-1}(x)$ be arbitrary. Since $p$ is a local homeomorphism, there is an open neighborhood $U \subseteq E$ of $t$ such that $p|_U : U \to p(U)$ is a homeomorphism. Now $(p|_U)^{-1} : p(U) \to U$ is a local section of $p$ whose value at $x$ is $t$. Thus, the map $s_x \mapsto s(x) : (\cal{S}_p)_x \to p^{-1}(x)$ is surjective. For injectivity, suppose $s_x \in (\cal{S}_p)_x$ is such that $s(x) = t$. Now $s^{-1}(U)$ is an open neighborhood of $x$, and we may assume without loss of generality that $s$ is defined on $s^{-1}(U)$. In particular, the image of $s$ is contained in $U$. Now $p|_U \circ s = \id_{s^{-1}(U)}$, so $s = (p|_U)^{-1}|_{s^{-1}(U)}$.
\end{proof}

Now we are ready to define the sheafification functor.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\cal{F} \in \PSh(X)$. The \emph{sheafification} of $\cal{F}$, denoted $\cal{F}^{++}$, is the sheaf of sections of the canonical projection $E_{\cal{F}} \to X$. In other words, the sheafification functor is the composite
    \[\PSh(X) \to \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X \to \Sh(X).\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
    There is a natural transformation $\eta_{\cal{F}} : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}^{++}$ given on local sections by $s \mapsto (u \mapsto (u, s_u))$. The induced map on stalks $\cal{F}_x \to \cal{F}^{++}_x$ is an isomorphism for all $x \in X$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    $\eta_{\cal{F}}$ is well-defined by \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:local section of presheaf is local section of espace etale}. Naturality is left as an exercise. The isomorphism on stalks is \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:stalks of local homeo are fibers}.
\end{proof}

Consequently, $\eta_{\cal{F}}$ is an isomorphism if and only if $\cal{F}$ is a sheaf.

\begin{theorem}
    \label{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves are local homeos}
    The espace étalé construction and the sheaf of sections construction give an equivalence of categories
    \[\Sh(X) \simeq \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    In one direction, $\eta_{\cal{F}} : \cal{F} \to \cal{F}^{++}$ is an isomorphism whenever $\cal{F}$ is a sheaf.

    In the other direction, let $p : E \to X$ be a local homeomorphism. We have a well-defined function $\beta : E_{\cal{S}_p} \to E$ given by
    $(x,s_x) \mapsto s(x)$
    which commutes with the projections $\pi : E_{\cal{S}_p} \to X$, $p : E \to X$. On fibers, this is exactly the isomorphism $\pi^{-1}(x) = (\cal{S}_p)_x \to p^{-1}(x)$ of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:stalks of local homeo are fibers}, so $\beta$ is a bijection. If we can establish that $\beta$ is continuous, we will know automatically that it is a local homeomorphism by \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:ToplhX is full in TopX}, from which it follows (since local homeomorphisms are open) that $\beta$ is an isomorphism in $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$.

    For this, let $V \subseteq E$ be open, and take an arbitrary element $(x, g) \in \beta^{-1}(V)$. Choose an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ and a local section $s \in \cal{S}_p(U)$ be such that $s_x = g$. Then $W := s^{-1}(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $x$. Now $(s|_W)[W]$ is open in $E_{\cal{S}_p}$, contains
    \[(x,g) = (x,s_x) = (x, (s|_W)_x),\]
    and is contained in $\beta^{-1}(V)$, since for all $w \in W$, we have
    \[\beta(w, (s|_W)_w) = (s|_W)(w) = s(w) \in V.\]
    This shows that $\beta^{-1}(V)$ is open, so $\beta$ is continuous.
\end{proof}

With the power of sheafification at our disposal, it becomes easy to define the left adjoint to $f_* : \Sh(X) \to \Sh(Y)$ for any continuous map $f : X \to Y$.

\begin{definition}
    Let $f : X \to Y$ be a continuous map of topological spaces. The left adjoint to $f_* : \Sh(X) \to \Sh(Y)$ is denoted $f^{-1}$, and given by the composite
    \[\Sh(Y) \hookrightarrow \PSh(Y) \xrightarrow{f^{-1}_\pre} \PSh(X) \xrightarrow{(-)^{++}} \Sh(X).\]
    We call this functor \emph{inverse image} or \emph{pullback} along $f$.
\end{definition}

To check that $f^{-1}$ is indeed left adjoint to $f_*$, we simply see that
\begin{align*}
    \Hom_{\Sh(X)}(f^{-1} \cal{F}, \cal{G}) & = \Hom_{\Sh(X)}((f^{-1}_\pre \cal{F})^{++}, \cal{G}) \\
                                           & \cong \Hom_{\PSh(X)}(f^{-1}_\pre \cal{F}, \cal{G})   \\
                                           & \cong \Hom_{\PSh(Y)}(\cal{F}, f_* \cal{G})           \\
                                           & \cong \Hom_{\Sh(Y)}(\cal{F}, f_* \cal{G})
\end{align*}
naturally in $\cal{F} \in \Sh(Y)$ and $\cal{G} \in \Sh(X)$.

\begin{proposition}
    $f^{-1} : \Sh(Y) \to \Sh(X)$ preserves stalks, i.e. for all points $x \in X$ there is a natural isomorphism
    \[(f^{-1} \cal{F})_x \cong \cal{F}_{f(x)}.\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    The inclusion $\Sh(Y) \to \PSh(Y)$, the functor $f^{-1}_\pre : \PSh(Y) \to \PSh(X)$, and the sheafification functor $(-)^{++} : \PSh(X) \to \Sh(X)$ all preserve stalks.
\end{proof}

It also becomes easy to prove important facts about the category of sheaves. Here are a few nice results that follow easily from the espace étalé construction.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:category of sheaves is cocomplete}
    $\Sh(X)$ is cocomplete. Moreover, the Yoneda embedding $y : \Open(X) \to \Sh(X)$ is a (not-necessarily free) cocompletion, i.e. it is fully faithful and its image generates $\Sh(X)$ under colimits (in a canonical way).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    For the first claim, take a diagram $D : \cal{I} \to \Sh(X)$ with $\cal{I}$ a small category. Form its colimit $\colim D$ in $\PSh(X)$, and then sheafify to get $(\colim D)^{++} \in \Sh(X)$. Since sheafification is a left adjoint functor, this computes $\colim_{i \in \cal{I}} D(i)^{++}$ in $\Sh(X)$. Since $D(i)$ is already a sheaf, we have a natural isomorphism $D(i) \cong D(i)^{++}$, and thus $(\colim D)^{++}$ computes the colimit of $D$ in $\Sh(X)$.

    For the second claim, we use the fact that the Yoneda embedding $y : \Open(X) \to \PSh(X)$ is a free cocompletion, i.e. every presheaf on $X$ is a small colimit (in a canonical way) of presheaves of the form $y(U)$ for $U \subseteq X$ open. Since sheafification is a left adjoint functor, it preserves colimits, so every sheaf on $X$ is a small colimit of sheaves of the form $y(U)^{++}$. But $y(U)$ is already a sheaf, so $y(U)^{++} \cong y(U)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    Let $\cal{F}$ be a sheaf on $X$. The slice category $\Sh(X)/\cal{F}$ is equivalent to the category of sheaves on the espace étalé $E_{\cal{F}}$, and in particular is complete and cocomplete.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    By \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves are local homeos}, we have an equivalence of categories $\Sh(X) \simeq \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$. Under this equivalence, the slice category $\Sh(X)/\cal{F}$ corresponds to the slice category $(\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X)/E_{\cal{F}}$, which is equivalent to $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/E_{\cal{F}}$ by inspection. Finally, by \Cref{thm:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:sheaves are local homeos} again, we have an equivalence of categories $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/E_{\cal{F}} \simeq \Sh(E_{\cal{F}})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    The slice category $\Sh(X)/y(U)$ is equivalent to $\Sh(U)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    The representable sheaf $y(U)$ corresponds to the inclusion $U \hookrightarrow X$ in $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    Let $f : X \to Y$ be a continuous function. The composite
    \[\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/Y \simeq \Sh(Y) \xrightarrow{f^{-1}} \Sh(X) \simeq \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X\]
    is given simply by pulling back morphisms along $f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    We have already seen (cf.\ \Cref{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:local homeos stable under pullback}) that local homeomorphisms are stable under pullback and that (cf.\ \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:ToplhX is full in TopX}) $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$ is a full subcategory of $\Top/X$, so ``pullback along $f$'' is a well-defined functor $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/Y \to \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$. Let this functor be denoted $f^*$ (just for this proof). We already know that $f^*$ agrees with $f^{-1}$ when $f = i_y : \pt \to Y$ is the inclusion of a point; in particular these ``fiber functors'' $i_y^* : \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/Y \to \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/\pt \simeq \Set$ (which agree with the stalk functors $i_y^{-1} : \Sh(Y) \to \Set$) preserve colimits. We now claim that $f^*$ preserves colimits in general.

    Let $D : \cal{I} \to \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/Y$ be a diagram. There is a natural map \[\colim (f^* \circ D) \to f^* (\colim D)\] in $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X$. Letting $x \in X$ be arbitrary and using the fact that fiber functors preserve colimits, we see that the induced map on fibers is isomorphic to the identity
    \begin{multline*}
        i_{f(x)}^*(\colim D) \cong \colim(i_{f(x)}^* \circ D) \cong \colim (i_x^* \circ f^* \circ D)
        \cong i_x^* (\colim (f^* \circ D))\\
        \to i_x^* (f^* (\colim D))
        \cong (f \circ i_x)^* (\colim D)
        = i_{f(x)}^* (\colim D).
    \end{multline*}
    Thus, the map $\colim (f^* \circ D) \to f^* (\colim D)$ is an isomorphism.

    We have now shown that $f^*$ preserves colimits. By \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:category of sheaves is cocomplete}, in order to show that $f^*$ and $f^{-1}$ agree, it now suffices to show that they agree (via a natural isomorphism) on the sheaves $y(U)$ for $U \subseteq Y$ open. For this, we note that $y(U) \in \Sh(Y)$ corresponds to the inclusion $U \hookrightarrow Y$ in $\Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/Y$. Pulling back this inclusion along $f$ gives the inclusion $f^{-1}(U) \hookrightarrow X$, which corresponds to the sheaf $y(f^{-1}(U)) \in \Sh(X)$. On the other hand,
    \begin{align*}
        \Hom_{\Sh(X)}(y(f^{-1}(U)),\cal{G}) & \cong \cal{G}(f^{-1}(U))                 \\
                                            & = (f_* \cal{G})(U)                       \\
                                            & \cong \Hom_{\Sh(Y)}(y(U),f_* \cal{G})    \\
                                            & \cong \Hom_{\Sh(X)}(f^{-1} y(U),\cal{G})
    \end{align*}
    establishes that $y(f^{-1}(U)) \cong f^{-1} y(U)$ naturally in $U \in \Open(Y)$.
\end{proof}

So, the preimage functor $f^{-1}$ is very easy to understand from the perspective of the espace étalé construction: it is just given by pullback! On the other hand, the direct image functor $f_*$ is kind of tricky to define from the perspective of the espace étalé. The good news is $f_*$ is easy to understand at the level of presheaves. Since we have both perspectives at our disposal, this is a good situation to be in.

We can also generalize \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:extension by zero} to arbitrary local homeomorphisms (instead of just open embeddings).

\begin{proposition}
    Let $f : X \to Y$ be a local homeomorphism. Then there is an adjoint triple
    \[f_! \dashv f^{-1} \dashv f_*\]
    between categories of sheaves, where $f_! : \Sh(X) \simeq \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/X \to \Top^{\mathrm{lh}}/Y \simeq \Sh(Y)$ is the functor given by postcomposition with $f$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    This is a general fact: postcomposition is always left adjoint to pullback (as a functor between slice categories), whenever pullback is well-defined.
\end{proof}

As mentioned at the start of this subsection, much of this discussion generalizes to presheaves with values in any algebraic category $\catD$. In particular, there is a sheafification functor $(-)^{++} : \PSh(X;\catD) \to \Sh(X;\catD)$ which is left adjoint to the inclusion $\Sh(X;\catD) \hookrightarrow \PSh(X;\catD)$, and an inverse image functor $f^{-1} : \Sh(Y;\catD) \to \Sh(X;\catD)$ which is left adjoint to the direct image functor $f_* : \Sh(X;\catD) \to \Sh(Y;\catD)$. In \Cref{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:abelian sheaves}, you will work through this generalization (and its consequences) for sheaves of abelian groups. In the next section of the notes, we will discuss a procedure for sheafification which works directly for sheaves with values in an arbitrary algebraic category $\catD$.

\begin{exercise}\label{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:manifolds as ringed spaces}
    A \emph{ringed space} is a pair $(X,\cal{O}_X)$ of a topological space $X$ and a sheaf $\cal{O}_X \in \Sh(X,\CRing)$, called the \emph{structure sheaf}. A \emph{morphism of ringed spaces} $(X,\cal{O}_X) \to (Y,\cal{O}_Y)$ is a pair $(f,f^\sharp)$ consisting of a continuous map $f : X \to Y$ together with a morphism $f^\sharp : \cal{O}_Y \to f_* \cal{O}_X$ of sheaves of rings on $Y$.

    For $M$ a smooth manifold, write $C^\infty_M$ for the sheaf $\Hom_\Man({-},\RR)$ of smooth functions on $M$ as in \Cref{exercise:chap:sheaves:sec:spaces:smooth structure determined by smooth functions}; note that $(M, C^\infty_M)$ is a ringed space.

    \[(U, \cal{O}_X|_U) \cong (\RR^n, C^\infty_{\RR^n}).\]
    A \emph{smooth space} is an $n$-dimensional smooth space for some $n \geq 0$. A morphism of smooth spaces is simply a morphism of ringed spaces.

    In this exercise, you will show that the category of smooth spaces is equivalent to the category of smooth manifolds. The upshot is that this gives a definition of smooth manifolds (not necessarily embedded in $\RR^n$!) which does \emph{not} require explicitly recording/choosing charts and atlases.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item Let $X$ be a smooth $n$-manifold.
              \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
                  \item Show that $(X,C^\infty_X)$ is an $n$-dimensional smooth space.
                  \item Show that $C^\infty_X$ is a sheaf of $\RR$-algebras\footnote{You may add the category of $\RR$-algebras to the list of ``algebraic categories''. Alternatively, a sheaf of $\RR$-algebras can be defined as a sheaf $\cal{F}$ of commutative rings together with a morphism $\underline{\RR} \to \cal{F}$, where $\underline{\RR}$ denotes the constant presheaf of commutative rings with value $\RR$.} in a unique way.\\
                        \textit{Hint:} Use the fact that the only ring endomorphism of $\RR$ is the identity.
              \end{enumerate}
        \item Let $(f,f^\sharp) : (\RR^n, C^\infty_{\RR^n}) \to (\RR^m, C^\infty_{\RR^m})$ be a morphism of ringed spaces.
              \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
                  \item Show that $f^\sharp$ is a morphism of sheaves of $\RR$-algebras.
                  \item For all $V \in \Open(\RR^m)$ and all $\varphi \in C^\infty_{\RR^m}(V)$, show that \[f^\sharp_V(\varphi) = \varphi \circ f|_{f^{-1}(V)}.\]
                        \textit{Hint:} Let $x \in f^{-1}(V)$ and $s \in \RR$ with $s \neq \varphi(f(x))$. It suffices to show that $(f^\sharp_V\varphi)(x) \neq s$. Use the fact that $\varphi - s$ is nonzero on some open neighborhood of $f(x)$.
              \end{enumerate}
        \item Let $f : X \to Y$ be a smooth map between smooth manifolds. Show that there is a unique morphism $f^\sharp : C^\infty_Y \to f_* C^\infty_X$ of sheaves of rings on $Y$, and that $f^\sharp$ happens to be a morphism of sheaves of $\RR$-algebras.
        \item Let $(X,\cal{O}_X)$ be an $n$-dimensional smooth space. Show that $X$ has a unique smooth $n$-manifold structure such that:
              \begin{description}
                  \item[$(\star)$] For every open set $U \subseteq X$ and every isomorphism of ringed spaces $(\RR^n, C^\infty_{\RR^n}) \xrightarrow{\cong} (U, \cal{O}_X|_U)$, the underlying map $\RR^n \to U$ is a diffeomorphism.
              \end{description}
              Then, show that $\cal{O}_X \cong C^\infty_X$ as sheaves of rings.
        \item Let $(f, f^\sharp) : (X,\cal{O}_X) \to (Y,\cal{O}_Y)$ be a morphism of smooth spaces. Show that $f : X \to Y$ is smooth, where we view $X$ and $Y$ as smooth manifolds by (d). Conclude that the category of smooth manifolds is equivalent to the category of smooth spaces.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{exercise}

\section{Sites}

We began the course by discussing ``sheaves on the category of sets'', which is certainly not a special case of $\Sh(X)$ for $X$ a topological space, since $\Set$ is not equivalent to $\Open(X)$ for any topological space $X$. In this section, we will discuss a generalization of the notion of sheaf which encompasses both sheaves on topological spaces and sheaves on the category of sets. For this, we must replace $\Open(X)$ and $\Set$ with a more general datum called a \emph{site}. A site is a category together with a collection of ``covering families'' -- these covering families specify exactly which equalizers should appear in the sheaf condition. It will turn out that every category can be equipped with a canonical choice of covering families, and that sheaves on $\Set$ or $\Open(X)$ with respect to these canonical covering families are exactly the sheaves we have already discussed.

\subsection{Grothendieck Topologies and the Sheaf Condition}

\begin{definition}
    Let $\catC$ be a category. A \emph{sieve} on an object $x \in \catC$ is a subfunctor $S \subseteq y(x)$ of the representable presheaf $y(x) = \Hom_\catC({-},x)$. That is, for all objects $c \in \catC$, we have a subset $S(c) \subseteq \Hom_\catC(c,x)$, and for all morphisms $f : c' \to c$ in $\catC$, we have $g \circ f \in S(c')$ for all $g \in S(c)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{warning}
    If $\catC$ is a large category, then $\PSh(\catC)$ is not necessarily a category in the usual sense (its hom-sets may in fact be proper classes). However, the notions of presheaf and subfunctor still make sense, so in particular it is fine to talk about sieves. I will try to be careful about size issues in what follows, but we'll be forced to return to this technical point again soon, and it is always worth thinking about which things are ``small'' and ``large''! This is a spot where the theory of Grothendieck universes would be very useful to make things precise, but I don't want to assume knowledge of Grothendieck universes in this course.
\end{warning}

A sieve is, in other words, a collection of morphisms into $x$ which is closed under precomposition. It's clear that the collection of sieves on $x$ is closed under arbitrary intersection (the empty intersection is the maximum sieve $y(x)$). Thus, we may talk about the sieve on $x$ generated by a collection of morphisms $\{f_\alpha\}_\alpha$ with codomain $x$, which is the smallest sieve containing all of the $f_\alpha$. Explicitly, the sieve generated by $\{f_\alpha\}_\alpha$ consists of all morphisms which factor through some $f_\alpha$, i.e.
\[S(c) = \{g \in \catC(c,x) : g = f_\alpha \circ h \text{ for some } \alpha \text{ and some }h : c \to \dom(f_\alpha)\}.\]

\begin{example}\label{example:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sieve generated by open cover}
    Let $X$ be a topological space, and let $U \subseteq X$ be an open set. Let $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ be an open cover of $U$. The sieve on $U \in \Open(X)$ generated by the inclusions $U_\alpha \hookrightarrow U$ is the subfunctor $S \subseteq y(U)$ defined by
    \[S(V) = \{V \hookrightarrow U : V \subseteq U_\alpha \text{ for some } \alpha\}.\]
\end{example}

If $S$ is a sieve on $x$ and $f : x' \to x$ is a morphism in $\catC$, we may define the \emph{pullback sieve} $f^* S$ on $x'$ by
\[(f^* S)(z) = \{g \in \catC(z,x') : f \circ g \in S(z)\}.\]

\begin{example}
    Let $X$ be a topological space, let $U \subseteq X$ be an open set, and let $\{U_\alpha\}_\alpha$ be an open cover of $U$. Let $S \subseteq y(U)$ be the sieve generated by the inclusions $U_\alpha \hookrightarrow U$. Let $V \subseteq U$ be an open subset. Then the pullback sieve $V^* S$ on $V$ is the sieve generated by the inclusions $V \cap U_\alpha \hookrightarrow V$, i.e.
    \[(V^* S)(W) = \{W \hookrightarrow V : W \subseteq V \cap U_\alpha \text{ for some } \alpha\}.\]
\end{example}

\begin{definition}
    Let $\catC$ be a category. A \emph{Grothendieck topology} $\cal{J}$ on $\catC$ is a choice of a collection\footnote{Potentially a proper class!} of sieves $\cal{J}(x)$ on each object $x \in \catC$, called the \emph{covering sieves}, such that:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(T\arabic*)]
        \item For all $S \in \cal{J}(x)$ and all morphisms $f : y \to x$, $f^* S \in \cal{J}(y)$.
        \item For all $S \in \cal{J}(x)$ and all sieves $T$ on $x$, if $f^* T \in \cal{J}(y)$ for all $f : y \to x$ in $S$, then $T \in \cal{J}(x)$.
        \item $y(x) \in \cal{J}(x)$ for all $x \in \catC$.
    \end{enumerate}
    A \emph{site} is a pair $(\catC, \cal{J})$ of a category $\catC$ and a Grothendieck topology $\cal{J}$ on $\catC$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}\label{example:canonical topology on OpenX}
    Let $X$ be a topological space. There is a Grothendieck topology $\cal{J}$ on $\Open(X)$ defined by declaring the covering sieves on $U$ to be precisely the sieves generated by open covers of $U$, as in \Cref{example:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sieve generated by open cover}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
    There is a Grothendieck topology on $\Set$ defined by declaring the covering sieves $S \in \cal{J}(X)$ to be precisely those for which the morphisms in $S$ are jointly surjective, i.e.\ for all $x \in X$ there exists some $f : Y \to X$ in $S(Y)$ and some $y \in Y$ such that $f(y) = x$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
    One sometimes sees sites defined in a slightly different way, in terms of \emph{Grothendieck pre-topologies}. Here, instead of specifying covering sieves, one specifies covering families $\{f_\alpha : x_\alpha \to x\}_\alpha$ of morphisms with codomain $x$. These covering families are required to satisfy the following axioms:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(PT\arabic*)]
        \item For all covering families $\{f_\alpha : x_\alpha \to x\}_\alpha$ and all morphisms $g : y \to x$, $\{x_\alpha \times_x y \to y\}_\alpha$ is a covering family.
        \item For all covering families $\{f_\alpha : x_\alpha \to x\}_\alpha$ and all collections of covering families $\{g_{\alpha,\beta} : x_{\alpha,\beta} \to x_\alpha\}_\beta$, the family $\{f_\alpha \circ g_{\alpha,\beta} : x_{\alpha,\beta} \to x\}_{\alpha,\beta}$ is a covering family.
        \item The singleton family $\{\id_x : x \to x\}$ is a covering family for all $x$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Any Grothendieck pre-topology generates a Grothendieck topology by declaring a sieve $S$ on $x$ to be covering if and only if it contains a covering family\footnote{It is worth taking the time to understand how the axioms PT1-PT3 translate to the axioms T1-T3 via this construction!}. There are two downsides to this approach:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Many distinct pre-topologies may generate the same topology, and the eventual definition of ``sheaves on a site'' that we will use only depends on the Grothendieck topology, not the pre-topology.
        \item The axioms of a Grothendieck pre-topology assume the existence of (at least some) pullbacks in $\catC$, while the axioms of a Grothendieck topology do not.
    \end{itemize}
\end{remark}

Suppose for a moment that $\catC$ is small. The Yoneda lemma says that
\[\cal{F}(x) \cong \Hom_{\PSh(\catC)}(y(x),\cal{F})\]
for all presheaves $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC)$. Consequently, for any sieve $S \subseteq y(x)$, we have a canonical map
\[\cal{F}(x) \cong \Hom_{\PSh(\catC)}(y(x),\cal{F}) \to \Hom_{\PSh(\catC)}(S,\cal{F}).\]
We say that $\cal{F}$ \emph{satisfies the sheaf condition with respect to $S$} or \emph{is local with respect to $S$} if this map is a bijection. We will write $\cal{F}(S)$ as shorthand for $\Hom_{\PSh(\catC)}(S,\cal{F})$.

If $\catC$ is not a small category, the version of the Yoneda lemma we have stated (\Cref{prop:Yoneda}) does not apply. However, much of it still holds:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It is still true that $\Hom(y(x),\cal{F}) \cong \cal{F}(x)$ naturally in presheaves $\cal{F}$ and objects $x$; in particular, $\Hom(y(x),\cal{F})$ is a set.
    \item It is still true that every presheaf $\cal{F}$ is a colimit of representable presheaves in a canonical way (this colimit is indexed by a potentially large category, but it does exist).
\end{itemize}
In this case, we can still obtain a canonical map $\cal{F}(x) \to \cal{F}(S) := \Hom(S,\cal{F})$ for any sieve $S$ on $x$, but $\cal{F}(S)$ may be a proper class. Still, it makes sense to ask whether or not $\cal{F}$ satisfies the sheaf condition with respect to $S$!

If $S$ is generated by a set of morphisms, then we can guarantee that $\cal{F}(S)$ is a set. Many sites encountered in practice have the property that all covering sieves are generated by sets.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:FS is a set for small sieves}
    Let $S$ be a sieve on $x \in \catC$. If $S$ is generated by a set of morphisms $\{f_i : c_i \to x\}_i$, then
    \[\cal{F}(S) := \Hom(S,\cal{F})\]
    is a set for any presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    The key is that, in this case, the morphism
    \[\coprod_i y(c_i) \to S\]
    determined by the elements $f_i \in S(c_i)$ is an epimorphism in $\PSh(\catC)$; i.e.\ it is a natural transformation whose components are all surjections. To see this, note that the image of this map $\coprod_i y(c_i) \to S$ is a well-defined subfunctor of $S$ (thus in particular a sieve on $x$) which contains all of the $f_i$; since $S$ is the smallest sieve containing all of the $f_i$, it follows that this image must be all of $S$. It follows that the induced map
    \[\cal{F}(S) = \Hom(S,\cal{F}) \to \Hom(\coprod_i y(c_i),\cal{F}) \cong \prod_i \Hom(y(c_i),\cal{F}) \cong \prod_i \cal{F}(c_i)\]
    is injective, and thus $\Hom(S,\cal{F})$ is a set.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sheaf condition as limit}
    Let $\catC$ be a category, and let $S$ be a sieve on $x \in \catC$. Let $S/x$ denote the full subcategory of $\catC/x$ spanned by the morphisms in $S$.

    Then, for any presheaf $\cal{F}$, elements of $\cal{F}(S)$ are in bijection with compatible families $(s_f)_{f \in S}$, where $s_f \in \cal{F}(c)$ for each $f : c \to x$ in $S$. Here compatibility means that for all morphisms $g : f' \to f$ in $S/x$, we have $\cal{F}(g)(s_f) = s_{f'}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Given $\alpha : S \to \cal{F}$, set $s_f := \alpha_c(f) \in \cal{F}(c)$ for each $f : c \to x$ in $S$. This is compatible by naturality of $\alpha$. In the other direction, given a compatible family $(s_f)_{f \in S}$, define $\alpha_c : S(c) \to \cal{F}(c)$ by $\alpha_c(f) := s_f$. These are the components of a natural transformation $\alpha : S \to \cal{F}$ by the compatibility condition.
\end{proof}

This is almost just saying that $\cal{F}(S)$ is the limit of the diagram
\[(\smallint S)^\op \cong (S/x)^\op \xrightarrow{\dom} \catC^\op \xrightarrow{\cal{F}} \Set,\]
coming from the fact that $S$ is the colimit of the diagram
\[\smallint S \cong S/x \xrightarrow{\dom} \catC \xrightarrow{y} \PSh(\catC).\]
The only trouble is that the indexing category $\int S$ is potentially large, and so the desired limit does not necessarily exist in $\Set$, as we have discussed. However, the colimit defining $S$ does exist in $\PSh$, and we can describe directly what elements of $\cal{F}(S) = \Hom(S,\cal{F})$ are by the universal property of colimits. This gives the statement in the proposition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sheaf condition as equalizer}
    Let $\catC$ be a category, and let $S$ be a sieve on $x \in \catC$. Assume that the slice category $\catC/x$ admits binary products\footnote{These are just pullback squares with $x$ in the corner.} and that $S$ is generated by a set of morphisms $\{f_i : c_i \to x\}_i$. Then $\cal{F}(S)$ is naturally isomorphic to the equalizer of the diagram
    \[\prod_{i} \cal{F}(c_i) \rightrightarrows \prod_{i,j} \cal{F}(c_i \times_x c_j).\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Recall from the proof of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:FS is a set for small sieves} that restriction along the $f_i$ gives an injection $\cal{F}(S) \hookrightarrow \prod_i \cal{F}(c_i)$; we must identify its image with the equalizer, and for this we use the description of \Cref{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sheaf condition as limit}.

    If $(s_f)_{f \in S}$ is a compatible family, then for all $i,j$ we have $f_i \circ p_1 = f_j \circ p_2$, where $p_1 : c_i \times_x c_j \to c_i$ and $p_2 : c_i \times_x c_j \to c_j$ are the canonical projections, so
    \[\cal{F}(p_1)(s_{f_i}) = s_{f_i \circ p_1} = s_{f_j \circ p_2} = \cal{F}(p_2)(s_{f_j}).\]

    Conversely, suppose $(s_i)_i$ satisfies $\cal{F}(p_1)(s_i) = \cal{F}(p_2)(s_j)$ for all $i,j$. Every $f : d \to x$ in $S$ factors as $f = f_i \circ h$ for some $i$ and some $h : d \to c_i$; set $s_f := \cal{F}(h)(s_i)$. This is independent of the factorization: if also $f = f_j \circ k$, then $(h,k)$ induces $r : d \to c_i \times_x c_j$ with $h = p_1 \circ r$ and $k = p_2 \circ r$, whence
    \[\cal{F}(h)(s_i) = \cal{F}(r)(\cal{F}(p_1)(s_i)) = \cal{F}(r)(\cal{F}(p_2)(s_j)) = \cal{F}(k)(s_j).\]
    The family $(s_f)_{f \in S}$ is compatible: if $g : f' \to f$ is a morphism in $S/x$ and $f = f_i \circ h$, then $f' = f_i \circ (h \circ g)$, so $s_{f'} = \cal{F}(h \circ g)(s_i) = \cal{F}(g)(s_f)$. Finally, taking $h = \id_{c_i}$ shows $s_{f_i} = s_i$.
\end{proof}

This understanding allows us to extend our sheaf condition so it applies to presheaves with values in any algebraic category.

\begin{definition}
    Let $\catC$ be a category, let $S$ be a sieve on $x \in \catC$, and let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. A presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC,\catD)$ is said to \emph{satisfy the sheaf condition with respect to $S$} (or be \emph{local with respect to $S$}) if its underlying $\Set$-valued presheaf (coming from the forgetful functor $\catD \to \Set$) is local with respect to $S$.
\end{definition}

We have same equalizer description of the sheaf condition in this more general setting, coming from the fact that limits in $\catD$ are computed in $\Set$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sheaf condition as equalizer in algebraic category}
    Let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. If $S$ is generated by a set of morphisms $\{f_i : c_i \to x\}_i$ and $\catC/x$ admits binary products, then $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC,\catD)$ is local with respect to $S$ if and only if
    \[\cal{F}(x) \to \prod_i \cal{F}(c_i) \rightrightarrows \prod_{i,j} \cal{F}(c_i \times_x c_j)\]
    is an equalizer diagram in $\catD$.
\end{proposition}

This allows us to define sheaves on a site with values in an algebraic category\footnote{Or, as before, any locally finitely presentable category, if you're willing to be more abstract.}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:chap:sheaves:sec:sites:sheaf}
    Let $(\catC, \cal{J})$ be a site, and let $\catD$ be an algebraic category. A presheaf $\cal{F} \in \PSh(\catC,\catD)$ is a \emph{sheaf} if it is local with respect to all covering sieves. We write $\Sh(\catC,\catD)$ for the full subcategory of $\PSh(\catC,\catD)$ spanned by the sheaves.
\end{definition}

Again, we warn that $\Sh(\catC,\catD)$ may fail to be a category in the traditional sense if $\catC$ is not small (because its hom-sets may be too large).
